One Year Rebuilding My Understanding of Maintainable Android Architecture

Over the last year, I’ve been rebuilding my understanding of maintainable Android development while working on a long-running messenger showcase project.

The project gradually became an exploration of:
- reducing coordination overhead in shared Android codebases,
- keeping architectural decisions consistent over time,
- aligning architecture with product requirements,
- and balancing delivery speed against long-term maintainability.

One of the biggest lessons was realizing that maintainable architecture is not created by adopting specific patterns, frameworks, or libraries. It emerges from making product rules, ownership boundaries, and architectural decisions explicit enough that multiple engineers can evolve the system predictably over time.

The post covers:
- feature slicing and ownership boundaries,
- architecture rules and ADRs,
- why requirements should drive architecture,
- how testing evolved into a staged verification pipeline,
- and how coding agents changed my workflow over time.
